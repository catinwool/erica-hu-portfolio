Buttercup: a game for half-formed curiosity

Product exploration
Learning, memory, play

Field research across the US, UK, EU, and China into AI-mediated lifelong learning.

Buttercup is a related question, not an earlier version of Glia. The product was built around education, not memory-keeping between people.

Scouting the players

Around 150 hours in the field: interviews, persona work, testing early concepts with a small group across the US, UK, EU, and China. Three kinds of players kept showing up.

The Collector. Curious about everything, organized about nothing. Scattered interests needed to add up to something before they felt like they counted.

The Wanderer. Mid-transition, frozen by how many directions were available. She wanted “a feeling of where I could get to” before she'd even start moving.

The Builder. More project ideas than time. Ambition wasn't the shortage. Permission to make the first step small enough to actually take was.

How to play

Lo-fi sketches became three named screens. Cosmos is the map: progress as a constellation forming. The Meadow is home base, built around the single thread worth returning to that day. Rabbit Hole is the sandbox for half-formed questions.

The game traveled further than I expected for something built solo. One player used it to plan a shift into data science. Another used it to build fluency in Spanish before a move. We ran workshops at a design academy in China, where people played the game on paper before opening the app.

The run that stayed with me longest broke the rules entirely. A player was recording conversations with her mother, not to study anything, so the two of them could understand each other better through what they remembered together. That thread became the seed of Glia.
