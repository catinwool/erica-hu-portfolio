At Inner Chapter I worked as a foresight and strategy researcher across cultural analysis, market research, audience insight, and experience strategy. Each engagement started with an ambiguous cultural or strategic question and moved toward insight teams could use in product, brand, or experience decisions.

LEGO Group — play and girlhood

Question. How do play, gender, and girlhood show up in children’s cultural worlds — and what does that mean for how a brand understands play?

Approach. Cultural research into play practices, representations of girlhood, and the meanings children and caregivers attach to toys and stories.

Outcome. Insights that informed how play and girlhood could be understood as lived culture, not only as market segments — feeding strategy and experience conversations for the brand.

Shanghai American School — education and endowment

Question. How should an international school think about education, community, and long-term endowment strategy in a changing cultural and institutional context?

Approach. Research combining stakeholder insight, cultural and institutional analysis, and foresight framing for education strategy.

Outcome. Direction for how endowment and education strategy could stay connected to the school’s community and values over time.

Vans — youth culture research

Question. What is shifting in youth culture that a brand rooted in skate and street culture needs to hear clearly?

Approach. Cultural and audience research into youth practices, identity, and brand meaning — listening for what felt authentic versus extractive.

Outcome. Cultural insight to inform brand and experience strategy without reducing youth culture to a trend report.

Chanel — perfume exhibition research and experience strategy

Question. How can a perfume exhibition hold cultural meaning, sensory experience, and brand narrative at once?

Approach. Research into exhibition experience, perfume culture, and how visitors move between story, object, and sensation.

Outcome. Experience strategy that treated the exhibition as a cultural encounter — not only a product showcase.
