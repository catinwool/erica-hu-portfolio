LEGO Group: girls foundational insights refresh

In 2019, LEGO had foundational research on girls. By 2023, Covid-era schooling, fiercer educational competition, and a more digital childhood had shifted how girls in China grow up, play, and imagine themselves.

The brief asked how to make the LEGO brand popular among girls in China, and what that should mean for communication and a multi-year girls strategy. The work was qualitative and girl-centric: ethnography that treated girlhood as lived culture, not only as a market segment.

Popularity alone proved too thin a metric. Desire for toys showed up across social currency, personal engagement, developmental value, and presence in daily life.

Direction: speak clearly to girls without flattening gender neutrality. Reframe creativity as guided imagination. Open construction into customization. Let LEGO live closer to lifestyle objects in girls’ everyday worlds.

Shanghai American School: endowment strategy

Is the Chinese market, and are Chinese parents, ready to contribute to endowment campaigns? If so, what should such a campaign focus on?

Five group discussions across Puxi and Pudong campuses with thirty parents, then a wider community survey. Conversations opened with parents’ hopes for their children’s development before testing structured endowment themes.

An endowment drive anchored in facilities and hardware would not excite participation. What parents could not simply buy was software: networks of people, mindsets, and cultural exposure that expand a child’s horizons beyond a privileged bubble.

Four themes followed: thought leadership, practical learning, creating community, and real-world education. Lead with software rather than buildings. In China, contributing connections and company resources can be a culturally fluent first step before financial endowment feels natural.
