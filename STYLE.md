# Portfolio writing style

Must-read before editing copy on this site (homepage, About, case studies, Writing).

## Voice

- Sound like Erica: specific, human, concrete. Prefer lived detail over agency jargon.
- One clear stake per title. One concrete method or outcome per supporting line.
- Do not flatten work into generic phrases like "turning insight into product direction" or "navigating how people play, learn, dress."

## Punctuation

- No em dashes (`—`). Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead.
- En dashes (`–`) are fine for date or time ranges (`2020–2023`, `4–5pm`), or write `to`.
- Prefer short sentences over stacked asides.

## Homepage work cards

- Title: name the project and the stake (`Glia: ambient AI for memory and friendship`).
- Support line: method + outcome, not a second slogan.
- Do not put research-hour totals on homepage teasers (`300+ hours`, `~150 hours`). On a card they read like total tenure. Keep hour counts on case study pages where context is clear.
- Emphasize that Glia is a **venture-backed startup** when naming it in intros and teasers.
- Client lists can sit in the support line; logos already carry recognition.

## Case studies

- Lead with the question or belief, then approach, then what changed or what shipped.
- Methods belong in a methods block; do not pad body copy with method laundry lists.
- Keep confidential client findings at public level; say "available on request" when needed.
- Match existing page structure: hero, optional TOC, short sections, insight pull quotes sparingly.

## Words to prefer / avoid

| Prefer | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| ethnography, in-home, contextual | "synergized insights," "holistic journey" |
| venture-backed startup (Glia) | "exploring" when the product is live |
| concrete outcomes (shipped, redesigned, informed strategy) | vague "execution" / "activation" without an object |

## Before shipping copy

1. Read the surrounding page and match its register.
2. Search for `—` and remove rhetorical em dashes.
3. Check homepage teasers for hour-counts and soft slogans.
4. Keep HTML structure and class names; change words, not layout, unless asked.
