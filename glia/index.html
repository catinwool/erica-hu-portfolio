Glia: a memory composer

A multiplayer shoebox for memories

Founder · Engineering, design, research
Present

Filmed, produced, written by Erica Hu

Glia is a place to keep the record of a relationship — stories, keepsakes, and the small things that make someone feel close again. You curate digital shoeboxes of moments and gift them to people you care about.

Memory has always been about stories and keepsakes: dried flowers, a boarding pass, letters and postcards, photos of toothy smiles, a grandmother’s recipe written down by hand. Glia is built for that kind of keeping — not as a feed to scroll, but as something you compose with attention.

Where it came from

Glia grew out of an earlier question I had been circling for years: how do you build software that pays close attention to a person over time? An earlier product concept, Buttercup, was framed around lifelong learning. In research and beta feedback, something else kept surfacing — people recording conversations with family not to study anything, but so they could understand each other better through what they remembered together.

That thread became Glia in its current form: a place to keep the record of a relationship, not a curriculum.

What I’m building

As founder I work across product, engineering, design, and research — shaping the interface language, the memory model, and the experience of composing and sharing a shoebox with someone else.

The work is guided by a few commitments: poetic computing, wonder, a library of life, organic interfaces that grow with you, and the belief that attention is all we have — spend it on what matters.
