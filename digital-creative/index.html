In-home, ride-along, and comparative studies for Lear, IKEA, Volvo, Tata, and Fretebras: from age-inclusive seats to AR wayfinding.

Note: Client deliverables remain confidential. What follows is a public overview of the questions, methods, and kinds of outcomes. Selected work available on request.

IKEA: in-store experience research & AR navigation

Research into how customers move through the store (self-checkout familiarity, queue behavior, co-worker support) informed concept work for in-store AR navigation.

Pair a map-based navigating screen with an AR mode for aisle-level guidance, backed by workshops and desirability studies.

Lear: age-inclusive design research 适老化设计

In aging societies such as Japan and China, longevity is reshaping what mobility has to feel like. For Lear, whose clients include Toyota, the question sits close to the product: seats, entry, comfort, and the small rituals of a daily ride.

The research mixed in-home interviews, ride-alongs in everyday vehicles, and test-rides (including a Mercedes-Maybach and a Toyota Sienna), with pressure-mat capture at each person’s most comfortable setting.

Needs sat on two planes at once: full-body physical support, and psychological stability in motion, including the ability to adjust without waiting for a family member. Inclusive design had to feel ordinary, not like a special chair that announces frailty.

Tata Group: EV insight research

What can Chinese EV markets and innovation teach product and strategy questions in India: what to learn from, what not to copy, and where local conditions demand a different design response?

Volvo: navigation design research

How drivers understand, use, and respond to navigation systems in real-world interaction: attention, trust, moments of confusion, and how interface decisions shape the experience of getting somewhere.

Fretebras: product research and design exploration

What could Fretebras learn from a comparable “Uber for Trucks” model, and which lessons transfer into product and operating decisions? Comparative study, expert interviews, and product design exploration grounded in that comparison.
